# Bench 2026-09-07 — Phase 1 batch latency

> **Clips note (public release).** These numbers were measured on an earlier
> version of `bench/clips/` that Chatterbox generated from the same texts. The
> clips shipped in this repository were regenerated with Kokoro (`af_heart`)
> and one sentence of `03-long.txt` was reworded, so a re-run will not match
> these tables to the millisecond or the exact WER.

Test machine: RTX 5070 Ti (16 GB), Ryzen 9 7950X. faster-whisper 1.2.1 / CTranslate2 4.8.2, Chatterbox
0.1.7, torch cu128. Corpus: `bench/` (5 clips, 5 texts). Numbers are the
median of 3 warm runs from `scripts/bench.py --spawn ...` (private daemon
per config, so the systemd unit is untouched); `first_ms` is the very
first request after start. `wall_ms` is client-side wall clock over
loopback HTTP; `decode/model/encode` are the daemon's own `timing_ms`.

Reproduce:

    scripts/bench.py --suite both --runs 3 --spawn VOICE_WARMUP=1
    scripts/bench.py --suite stt --runs 3 --spawn WHISPER_COMPUTE_TYPE=int8_float16

## STT (final config: float16, beam 1, VAD, no conditioning, batched > 30 s)

| clip | audio_s | first_ms | wall_ms | decode_ms | model_ms | rtf | wer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01-short.ogg | 2.49 | 130 | 124 | 30.3 | 92.5 | 0.05 | 0.00 |
| 02-ten-sec.ogg | 11.3 | 183 | 183 | 38.3 | 143 | 0.02 | 0.12 |
| 03-long.ogg | 37.0 | 360 | 360 | 63.8 | 294 | 0.01 | 0.05 |
| 04-silence.ogg | 3.01 | 32.3 | 49.0 | 41.7 | 6.30 | 0.02 | 0.00 |
| 05-wav-16k.wav | 11.3 | 183 | 181 | 32.0 | 148 | 0.02 | 0.00 |

The "WER" on 02/03 is number formatting ("twelve seconds" → "12 seconds",
"three gigabytes" → "3GB"); every word is right. It is a tripwire, not a
score — the clips are synthetic (Chatterbox reading the texts), so real
voice-note WER will be worse and is not what this measures.

Where the time goes for a 10 s clip: ~40 ms ffmpeg decode (process spawn
dominates; the same for 2 s and 11 s), ~140 ms model, ~0 ms HTTP. The
README's Phase 0 number (5.8 s for a 5 s clip) was the cold first call;
warm was always sub-second. The real fix here is the warm-up, not the
knobs.

### int8_float16 vs float16

| clip | fp16 model_ms | int8 model_ms | Δ | wer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01-short | 92 | 83 | -10 % | same |
| 02-ten-sec | 140 | 127 | -9 % | same |
| 03-long | 295 | 269 | -9 % | same |
| 05-wav-16k | 147 | 134 | -9 % | same |

VRAM: 8842 → 7882 MB used with STT resident alone (-960 MB). Transcripts
identical on this corpus. Decision: stay on float16 by default. 13 ms on a
10 s clip is not perceivable and the quality risk on real Swedish/English
voice notes is unmeasured; int8_float16 is the switch to flip if VRAM gets
tight when Phase 2/3 adds models (`WHISPER_COMPUTE_TYPE=int8_float16`).

### BatchedInferencePipeline for > 30 s

| clip (37 s) | model_ms |
|---|---|
| sequential (`WHISPER_BATCH_ABOVE_S=9999`) | 356 |
| batched, batch_size 8 | 294 |

-17 % on a 37 s clip, same transcript. Kept, threshold 30 s (one whisper
window; below it batching is pure overhead). Gains grow with length; a 2 min
voice note is where this matters.

### condition_on_previous_text=False

No measurable speed change on this corpus (expected: it only affects
> 30 s clips and mostly guards against hallucination cascades). Kept off
because the failure mode it prevents is the ugly one.

## TTS (final config: chunk ≤ 300 chars, first chunk ≤ 80 chars, one ffmpeg)

| text | chars | audio_s | first_ms | wall_ms | first_chunk_ms | model_ms | encode_ms | rtf |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01-short.txt | 53 | 2.84 | 788 | 788 | 736 | 736 | 42.1 | 0.28 |
| 02-medium.txt | 240 | 11.9 | 3361 | 3139 | 801 | 3049 | 88.9 | 0.26 |
| 03-long.txt | 524 | 25.3 | 5812 | 6170 | 531 | 6014 | 156 | 0.24 |
| 04-numbers-and-code.txt | 220 | 16.6 | 3942 | 3942 | 1377 | 3825 | 116 | 0.24 |
| 05-expressive.txt | 235 | 12.2 | 2828 | 2898 | 612 | 2820 | 88.6 | 0.24 |

Chatterbox is ~linear in characters at ~12 ms/char (RTF ≈ 0.25, stable
across lengths). Encode (libopus via one ffmpeg) is 3 % of the total.
Nothing to gain in the batch path from here except a faster engine; the
next real win is streaming chunks as they finish (Phase 3), which the
first-chunk cap already sets up.

### First-chunk cap sweep (`VOICE_TTS_FIRST_CHUNK_CHARS`)

| cap | 02-medium first chunk | 03-long | 04-numbers | 05-expressive | total Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300 (= no cap) | 2654 | 2307 | 3608 | 2816 | — |
| 120 | 775 | 521 | 1807 | 1170 | +2–5 % |
| 60 | 772 | 559 | 1191 | 591 | +5–8 % |
| 80 (chosen) | 801 | 531 | 1377 | 612 | +3–5 % |

Each extra chunk costs a fixed ~100–200 ms of generate() overhead, so a
smaller first chunk trades a little total time for time-to-first-audio.
80 is the knee. 04-numbers stays > 1 s because Chatterbox spells out
"429", "VOICE_TIMEOUT", "32s" — more phonemes per char, not a chunker
problem.

## Warm-up

`VOICE_WARMUP=1` (default) runs after load: STT — 1 s noise through the
plain path, 20 s through the batched path, 1 s through VAD (~360 ms total);
TTS — one short sentence (~620 ms). Cost is paid once at daemon start, which
systemd already tolerates (`TimeoutStartSec=900`).

## Changes that landed with this

- `timing_ms {decode, model, encode, total, request}` on every `/stt`
  response body and in the `X-Timing-Ms` header on `/tts` (body is audio);
  `/health` carries load/warm-up/last-request timings. One JSON line per
  request on logger `voice-daemon.req`.
- Engine seam: STT takes float32 PCM at `engine.sample_rate`, the daemon
  decodes with ffmpeg (invariant 4 now holds for input too; before,
  faster-whisper spawned its own ffmpeg via PyAV). TTS engines expose
  `max_chars`; the daemon owns sentence chunking so it can time the first
  chunk. Both engines have `warmup()`.
- `VOICE_TTS_MAX_TEXT_CHARS` (4000) hard cap per request → HTTP 400.
- `engines/whisper_ct2.py` imports torch before CTranslate2 touches CUDA:
  there is no system CUDA toolkit here, the torch wheels carry libcublas,
  and STT-only daemons (`VOICE_RESIDENT=stt`) crashed on first encode
  without it. Phase 0 never hit this because Chatterbox loaded first.

## Not done, on purpose

Streaming, server VAD, TTS caching (plan says so). No Hermes contract
change: `bin/hermes-stt` / `bin/hermes-tts` are untouched and still work.
